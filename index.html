About me

I am a senior at the University of Washington studying physics, astronomy, and philosophy. I study stellar streams, the thin trails of disrupted dwarf galaxies and star clusters.

Streams record the gravitational potential of their host galaxy as they orbit, and give us information on dark matter substructure around the Milky Way. Identifying and characterizing a large population of streams is one of the few ways to test what dark matter does on small scales.

At UW's DiRAC Institute I'm part of the stream team, working with Nora Shipp and Peter Ferguson on streams in the DECam Local Volume Exploration survey (DELVE). I built a pipeline to search full survey catalogs for faint streams, and found a diffuse extension of the Sutlej stream that may be debris from the ultra-faint satellite Tucana III.

In early 2026 I spent three months in La Serena, Chile, through the NOIRLab REU, hunting for faint dwarf galaxies around the spiral galaxy NGC 6744 with Bryan Miller at Gemini Observatory.

I'm applying to PhD programs for fall 2027. I aim to do cosmology with all sorts of faint objects, and eventually help lead the surveys that find them.

Research

Finding faint streams in DELVE

DELVE's catalogs are too large to search the way smaller surveys were searched, so I built a pipeline with LSDB and the HATS format that runs isochrone-based matched filters over the full catalog without loading it into memory. The same design scales to LSST.

I found a diffuse extension of the Sutlej stream whose stars match the population of Tucana III, a disrupting ultra-faint satellite. I'm testing whether Sutlej is Tucana III debris using isochrone fits, distances from blue horizontal branch and RR Lyrae stars, Gaia proper motions, and disruption models that include the Large Magellanic Cloud and the Galactic bar. The paper goes out in November 2026.

Weighing a dark subhalo with a kinked stream

The ATLAS–Aliqa Uma stream has a kink that a passing dark matter subhalo may have caused. I built a probabilistic membership model that combines photometry, Gaia proper motions, and spectroscopic members to decide which stars belong to the stream. Those members feed a simulation-based inference of the subhalo's properties, in a paper led by L. C. Marin for the LSST Dark Energy Science Collaboration.

Faint dwarf galaxies around NGC 6744

NGC 6744 is a nearby spiral much like the Milky Way, and its faintest satellites test the same ideas about dark matter as our own. I searched for ultra-faint and diffuse dwarf galaxies in its group using Gemini spectra whose signal sat barely above the noise, and assessed methods to recover that signal, focusing on spectra in the Calcium II infrared triplet regime. The project continues with Gemini observations in semester 2026B, and I'm presenting it at AAS 249 in January!
